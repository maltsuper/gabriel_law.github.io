Meddle
I like this/10

18.07.26 - First Listen
Two songs in, it's giving defiant pilgrimage. As though one has been exiledand now proceeds to travel whilst spreading the burden of their pain upon the earth. But they eventually chill out afterwards. I like how some Pink Floyd songs has really long songs. I feel like I really get absorbed in them since I'm not constantly jumping from vibe to vibe ten times across an hour or so. I really like the cover art. The pastel blue feels very welcoming, pure, and kind, whilst the dirty blood-stained red has a more off-putting air and a more grimy-distored feel to it. But, their combination feels very nice. Nevermind, I'm on song 4 and everything feels a lot more positive and whimsical. Maybe this is the dichotomy displayed in the album cover? I won't take back my previous observations but simply make them more 'acute'. By the end of the album there isn't so much of an 'aggresive' or 'confrontational' vibe as an eerie and sinister one. I think that's the nuance I missed initially. Towards the end, the mood started to be that of an accomplished, yet introspective, success story. The calm after the storm one might say. The quiet after a wonderful victory, which vividly acknowledges that the drive and vigour have been left in days behind. The knowledge that 'The Hero's Journey' is now just 'The Hero's Tale'. I like this album.

21.07.26 - Several-th listen
For the past few days I've been strugglin to articulate my thoughts on this album. I've been slowly accumulating an idea of what it might be that attracts me to this album and this morning I think I managed to understand it. This album lacks any particular 'special' moments--and I like that. It's a story in the realest form. A journey. A pilgrimage. A shared passage of time, where one moves and evolves through space. This album doesn't need any focal points at all. It's a fluid journey. It guides you through many emotional states, and the fact that these emotional moments are not over-exagerated, for me, makes it that much more honest and true to life. In a real pilgrimage one is not typically met with a serial killer, a dragon, or a mind-altering final boss. Just a journey which has its ups and downs which, eventually, will all come to an end, and I think this album encapsulates this idea. It's not trying to break-down a monolith of societal ideals or attack a group of mislead individuals. To me, it's a solo adventurer on a prilgrimage--and that's all I wish it to be. I like this album.
